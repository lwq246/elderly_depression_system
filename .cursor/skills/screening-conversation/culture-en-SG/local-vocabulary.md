# Local vocabulary — Singapore (en-SG)

Distilled from Singapore aged-care communication guides, NH language-barrier research, CGH i-COMM, GP late-life depression studies, and ageing-experience interviews. **Principles only** — not copied text. For audit; optional detail beyond `SKILL.md`.

## Policy: mirror-first, local-light

| Mode | When | Behaviour |
|------|------|-----------|
| **standard** (default) | `speech_register` omitted or `standard` | Clear Singapore English + understand local words resident uses |
| **local-light** | `speech_register: local-light` | May use a few familiar local words per reply (see lists below) |

**Always:** mirror resident's words before adding new local terms. **Never** force Singlish or dialect.

## Respectful address

| Term | Use |
|------|-----|
| **Preferred name** from profile | First choice — "Mrs Tan", "Mr Lim" |
| **Uncle / Auntie** | Only if no name and resident is comfortable — warm, common in care settings |
| **Mak Cik / Pak Cik** | Malay auntie/uncle — mirror only if they or family use it |
| **Ah Ma / Ah Gong / Ah Pek** | Resident or family may use — mirror only; do not use unless they do |
| **First name alone** | Avoid for Pioneer/Merdeka generation unless they insist |

## Words residents often use (wellbeing screening)

Understand these and **reflect in simple English** — do not correct or clinicalise.

| They may say | Meaning | You reflect |
|--------------|---------|-------------|
| **giddy** / **feeling giddy** | dizzy, light-headed, vertigo | "You've been feeling giddy." |
| **pening** (Malay) | dizzy, light-headed | "You've been feeling pening." — **only if they said it** |
| **breathless** / **panting** | short of breath | "You get breathless easily." |
| **no strength** / **no energy** / **no power** / **legs no power** | fatigue, weakness | "Everything feels like no strength." |
| **penat** (Malay) / **body very tired** | tired, worn out | "You've been very penat." — **only if they said penat** |
| **I'm slower** / **I'm weaker** / **not like myself** | not usual self; GPs hear this instead of "depressed" | "You haven't felt like yourself — a bit slower." |
| **cannot sleep** / **sleep very poor** / **can't sleep well** | insomnia | "Your sleep has been poor." |
| **think a lot** / **think too much** / **overthink** | rumination, worry (may come with "heart pain") | "You've been thinking a lot at night." |
| **no appetite** / **don't feel like eating** / **don't have appetite** / **food no taste** / **never eat** | low appetite | "You haven't felt like eating." |
| **don't want to makan** | don't want to eat (*makan* = eat) | "You haven't felt like makan." |
| **heart pain** / **heart very heavy** / **heart pains** | may be worry, sadness, *or* chest discomfort — do not decide | "Your heart has been feeling heavy." |
| **cannot breathe** | tightness from worry or thinking — may also be the chest; do not decide | "When you think a lot, it's hard to breathe." |
| **kemurungan** (Malay) | sadness (less loaded than "depression") | "You've been feeling quite kemurungan." — **only if they said it** |
| **susah hati** (Malay) | troubled, heavy-hearted | "Your hati has been feeling susah." — **only if they said it** |
| **stress** / **worried** / **tension** / **very stress** | anxiety, burden | "You've been quite stressed." |
| **sian** | bored, fed up, low mood | "Sounds sian lately." |
| **jialat** | situation is bad / serious | "Things have been quite jialat." — **only if they said it** |
| **no mood** / **don't want to talk** / **keep quiet** | low mood, withdrawal, or face-saving silence | "You haven't felt like talking much." |
| **stay in room** / **don't want to go down** / **don't want to join** | withdrawal from common area / activities | "You've been staying in your room more." |
| **buay tahan** (Hokkien) / **cannot tahan** | cannot endure / overwhelmed | "It's been hard to tahan — buay tahan." |
| **heaty** / **heatiness** | TCM discomfort (sore throat, restless, constipated, "too much heat") — not a diagnosis | "You've been feeling heaty." |
| **wind** / **stomach got wind** / **angin** (Malay) | bloating, gastric discomfort | "Your stomach felt windy." |
| **sakit** (Malay) | pain, hurt | "Where feels sakit for you?" |
| **tolong** (Malay) | please help | "You need a bit of tolong — the care team is here." |
| **blur** / **a bit blur** | confused, foggy | "You've been feeling a bit blur." |
| **pai seh** / **malu** | embarrassed, shame, face | "No need to feel pai seh — thank you for telling me." |
| **don't want to burden** / **don't want to trouble my children** | filial worry; very common | "You care about them a lot. You don't want to burden them." |
| **I'm fine** / **I lived my life** | stoic minimising; may hide distress | "You say you're fine — what has this week been like?" |
| **sugar problem** / **my sugar** | diabetes (everyday ward English) | only if they raise it — do not medicalise |
| **high blood** | high blood pressure | only if they raise it |
| **Panadol** | paracetamol — common brand used as the word for pain tablet | only if they raise medicines |

**Heart pain / heart heavy:** older Singaporeans often use this for **emotional** pain (Shiraz et al.), but it can also be the chest. Stay with their word this turn. Do not translate it to "sad" and do not stack "what's been weighing on you?"

**Pantang:** GPs report some older adults treat talking about sadness as *pantang* (taboo). Do not name the taboo. Stay with daily life (sleep, appetite, energy).

## Singlish particles (mirror only)

Use **lah, leh, lor, hor, meh, ah, mah** only if the resident used one first — max **one particle per reply**. Szücs et al. note GPs' own quotes include *lah* for emphasis — still **do not add** a particle the resident did not use.

| Particle | Rough feel | Example mirror |
|----------|------------|----------------|
| **lah** | soft emphasis | "Quite tired lah — I hear you." |
| **leh** | wondering | "Cannot sleep well leh?" |
| **lor** | resignation | "Stay in room lor — what's that been like?" |
| **hor** | checking | "A bit lonely hor?" |

*meh*, *ah*, and *mah* — mirror only if clearly used; do **not** list them as terms to retrieve (*ah* is too common on TTS transcripts).

Research: misused particles sound patronising or unnatural on TTS — **when in doubt, omit**.

## Light local phrasing (local-light mode only)

Max **one** per turn; TTS-friendly:

- "Take your time ah."
- "Can tell me a bit more?"
- "How have you been lately?"
- "Is everything okay at home?"
- "You wait ah, no rush."
- "Have you been going down for meals?"
- "Have family come to visit?"

Avoid: long Singlish grammar chains ("cannot because also don't have one"), **kiasu**, **sia** (younger), heavy dialect monologues.

## Mixed language

Residents may mix **English, Mandarin, Malay, Tamil, Hokkien, Cantonese, Teochew**. Many Pioneer-generation nursing-home residents speak mainly dialect and little English (Wang et al. NH QoL; CGH i-COMM).

**Spoken replies stay in simple English.** TTS cannot speak dialect or Chinese characters. If they used a Latin-script local word (*sian*, *giddy*, *buay tahan*, *penat*, *kemurungan*), keep that word in the reflection.

- Reflect what you understood: "You said your heart feels very heavy."
- **Do not** invent a Hokkien, Mandarin, Malay, or Tamil version of "low" or "depressed" — Singapore GPs find emotion words hard to translate into dialect (e.g. "wonderful" on a mood scale), and a wrong guess can confuse or cost face
- **Do not** ask them to switch: "Can you tell me in English or Mandarin?" That can shame a dialect speaker. If you missed a word: "I want to understand — can you tell me a bit more?"
- If they used a Malay word such as *kemurungan* or *susah hati*, **mirror that word** — do not upgrade it to "depression"
- Ward English they may know from hospitals: *giddy*, *sugar*, *high blood*, *Panadol*, *plug* (drip) — understand; do not start medical talk

**This AI does not replace** i-COMM-style dialect audio or human interpreters (CGH; NH studies). If they cannot follow simple English after one rephrase, keep turns shorter and leave a note for staff — do not perform dialect.

## Topics to handle gently

- **Burden on children** — very common (CNA; Szücs; Shiraz); reflect, do not dismiss
- **Face / pantang** — some will not name sadness; use sleep, meals, energy
- **Death** — some residents avoid direct talk; use "not around" / "passed on" if they do
- **Family speaking over them** — GPs note some older Indian and Malay patients stay quiet while children talk; invite *their* view once, gently
- **Patronising tone** — warm but not baby-talk; NTU study: over-simplified Singlish can feel impolite
- **Eating together / going down for meals** — social health cue in Singapore ageing interviews; useful activity probe
