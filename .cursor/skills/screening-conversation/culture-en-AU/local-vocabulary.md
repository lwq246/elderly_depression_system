# Local vocabulary — Australia (en-AU)

Distilled from Cultural Diversity in Ageing practice guides, NSW Multicultural Health, and Australian aged-care plain-English standards. **Principles only** — not copied text.

## Policy: plain English first, local-light optional

Australian aged-care guidance prioritises **plain English** for CALD residents (reading age ~9; avoid colloquialism that fails to translate).

| Mode | When | Behaviour |
|------|------|-----------|
| **standard** (default) | `speech_register` omitted or `standard` | Plain Australian English; understand local words residents use |
| **local-light** | `speech_register: local-light` | Familiar Aussie everyday words — max one colloquialism per turn |

**Always:** mirror resident's words first. **Never** blokey slang every sentence.

## Respectful address

| Term | Use |
|------|-----|
| **Preferred name** | "Mrs Tan", "Mr Smith" — first choice |
| **Mate** | Avoid unless resident uses it first |
| **Love / darling** | Some rural/older staff use — **avoid in AI** unless facility SOP allows |

## Words residents often use (wellbeing screening)

| They may say | Meaning | You reflect |
|--------------|---------|-------------|
| **been going** / **how you going** | how are you | "How have you been going?" |
| **crook** | unwell, low | "You've been feeling a bit crook." |
| **flat** / **a bit flat** | low mood | "You've been feeling flat." |
| **down in the dumps** | low mood | "A bit down in the dumps." |
| **knackered** / **worn out** / **buggered** (mild) | exhausted | "You feel worn out." |
| **off your food** | low appetite | "You've been off your food." |
| **not sleeping** / **crappy sleep** | poor sleep | "Sleep hasn't been great." |
| **a bit quiet** / **keeping to yourself** | withdrawal | "You've been keeping to yourself." |
| **she'll be right** | minimising | Reflect without arguing — one gentle probe |
| **hard yakka** | effort, struggle | "Everything's been hard yakka." |

## Australian daily-life words (local-light)

Safe for TTS in moderation:

| Word | Context |
|------|---------|
| **yarn** | friendly talk — "a quiet yarn about how you've been" |
| **cuppa** | tea/coffee — "still enjoy a cuppa in the lounge?" |
| **morning tea** | social routine — common in RAC |
| **lounge** | common sitting area |
| **garden walk** | activity probe |
| **footy** | only if they mention TV/sport |
| **arvo** | afternoon — sparingly; prefer "this afternoon" for clarity |

## Opener colloquialisms

| Style | Example |
|-------|---------|
| **Local-light** | "G'day, Mrs Tan… quiet yarn…" (`greeting.txt`) |
| **Standard** | "Hello, Mrs Tan… friendly chat…" — swap if resident seems formal |

Follow their cue after turn one.

## CALD caution

Many residents are **not** native Australian English speakers (AIHW RAC language data):

- Prefer **concrete** words: meals, sleep, family, garden
- Avoid puns, rhyming slang, niche rural-only terms
- If they use Italian/Greek/Cantonese etc., reflect in **simple English**
- Professional interpreters for complex needs — TIS **131 450** (staff arrange, not AI)

## Men's / stoic presentation

May say **"I'm fine"**, **"don't get lonely"**, or describe **back pain, tiredness** before mood — use somatic entry; one probe only.
