# Local vocabulary — Australia (en-AU)

Distilled from Cultural Diversity in Ageing practice guides, NSW Multicultural Health, healthdirect, ANDC/Macquarie Australian English, PalliAGED, and older-adult mental health research. **Principles only** — not copied text.

## Policy: plain English first, local-light optional

Australian aged-care guidance prioritises **plain English** for CALD residents (reading age ~9; avoid colloquialism that fails to translate).

| Mode | When | Behaviour |
|------|------|-----------|
| **standard** (default) | `speech_register` omitted or `standard` | Plain Australian English; understand local words residents use |
| **local-light** | `speech_register: local-light` | Familiar Aussie everyday words — max one colloquialism per turn |

**Always:** mirror resident's words first. **Never** blokey slang every sentence.

## Respectful address

| Term | Use |
|------|-----|
| **Preferred name** | "Mrs Tan", "Mr Smith" — first choice |
| **Mate** | Avoid unless resident uses it first |
| **Love / darling** | Some rural/older staff use — **avoid in AI** unless facility SOP allows |
| **Good on you** | Warm affirmation when they share something hard — not cheerleading |

## Words residents often use (wellbeing screening)

Understand these and **reflect in simple English** — do not correct or clinicalise. Prefer the milder reflection if the resident used a strong word.

| They may say | Meaning | You reflect |
|--------------|---------|-------------|
| **been going** / **how you going** | how are you | "How have you been going?" |
| **not myself** / **not like myself** / **not quite myself** | off, not usual self | "You haven't been feeling quite yourself." |
| **crook** / **feeling crook** / **a bit crook** | unwell, low, or injured | "You've been feeling a bit crook." |
| **crook as a dog** / **crook as Rookwood** | very unwell (Rookwood = Sydney cemetery) | "You've been feeling really crook." |
| **under the weather** / **off colour** | mildly unwell | "You've been a bit under the weather." |
| **lurgy** | a bug, virus, feeling sick | "You've had a bit of a lurgy." |
| **flat** / **a bit flat** | low mood or low energy | "You've been feeling flat." |
| **a bit down** / **feeling down** / **down in the dumps** | low mood | "You've been feeling a bit down." |
| **a bit blue** | low mood | "You've been feeling a bit blue." |
| **not coping** / **not coping too well** | struggling; often said instead of "depressed" | "It sounds like it's been hard to cope." |
| **knackered** / **worn out** / **run down** / **shattered** / **whacked** | exhausted | "You feel worn out." |
| **buggered** (mild) / **stuffed** (mild) | exhausted — do not escalate the swear | "You feel worn out." |
| **can't be bothered** / **can't be stuffed** | no drive; withdrawal | "You haven't felt like doing much." |
| **off your food** / **gone off my food** / **off my food** | low appetite | "You've been off your food." |
| **not eating** / **picking at food** | poor intake | "You haven't been eating much." |
| **not sleeping** / **crappy sleep** / **rotten sleep** | poor sleep | "Sleep hasn't been great." |
| **tossing and turning** / **up all night** / **wide awake** | insomnia, broken sleep | "You've been tossing and turning." |
| **aches and pains** / **everything hurts** / **crook knee** / **bung knee** / **bung hip** | pain; joint trouble (*bung* = not working) | "The aches and pains have been getting to you." |
| **dicky ticker** / **wonky ticker** | heart trouble — medical or worry; do not diagnose | "Your ticker's been worrying you." |
| **a bit quiet** / **keeping to yourself** / **keeping to myself** | withdrawal | "You've been keeping to yourself." |
| **not going out** / **not leaving the house** / **staying in** | withdrawal, isolation | "You've been staying in more." |
| **nothing to do** / **bored stiff** / **fed up** | boredom / low interest | "There's been nothing much to do." |
| **lonely** / **a bit lonely** / **rattling around** | loneliness | "It's been a bit lonely." |
| **she'll be right** / **she'll be apples** / **no worries** | minimising; "all fine" | Reflect without arguing — one gentle probe |
| **I'm fine** | stoic minimising | "You say you're fine — what's the week really been like?" |
| **don't get lonely** | stoic denial of loneliness | "You say you don't get lonely — how have the days felt?" |
| **doing it tough** / **tough time** / **hard yakka** | struggling; effort | "You've been doing it tough." |
| **had a gutful** / **had enough** / **over it** | fed up, overwhelmed | "You've had enough of it." |
| **cranky** / **snappy** / **irritable** / **a bit snaky** | irritable (common in older men before mood words) | "You've been feeling a bit cranky." |
| **on edge** / **a bit nervy** / **worried sick** / **packing it** | anxious, wound up | "You've been on edge." |
| **teary** / **weepy** | tearful | "You've been feeling teary." |
| **missing the kids** / **the kids are interstate** / **grandkids far away** | family distance, loneliness | "You miss the kids." |
| **black dog** | low mood (Beyond Blue / public metaphor) — **only if they said it** | "The black dog's been around." |

**Do not introduce** these yourself: *crook as Rookwood* (local; fails CALD), *buggered*, *stuffed*, *black dog*, *packing it*, *snaky*, *Rookwood*. Mirror only.

**Ambiguous — do not treat as low mood:** *flat out* (busy), *gone off* alone (food spoiled), *go crook at* (get angry at someone), *yarn* (a chat, not a symptom).

## Australian daily-life words (local-light)

Safe for TTS in moderation. Use **one** per turn in local-light; otherwise prefer the plain column.

| Word | Context | You may say (local-light) |
|------|---------|---------------------------|
| **yarn** | friendly talk | "a quiet yarn about how you've been" |
| **cuppa** | tea/coffee | "still enjoy a cuppa in the lounge?" |
| **morning tea** | social routine in RAC | "Have you been going to morning tea?" |
| **lounge** | common sitting area | "Have you been sitting in the lounge?" |
| **garden walk** | activity probe | "Have you been out for a garden walk?" |
| **lawn bowls** | common activity | only if they mention bowls |
| **footy** | AFL/NRL on TV | only if they mention sport |
| **RSL** | club, mates, Anzac social life | only if they mention it |
| **men's shed** | activity/connection for some men | only if they mention it |
| **chemist** | pharmacy (AU everyday) | only if they raise medicines |
| **GP** | doctor | only if they raise the doctor |
| **arvo** | afternoon | sparingly; prefer "this afternoon" |
| **nanna nap** | daytime sleep | "Have you been needing a nanna nap?" |
| **grandkids** | family connection | "Have the grandkids been in touch?" |
| **in here** | the facility | use their word for the place |
| **lifestyle** | activities staff/program in RAC | "Have you joined any of the lifestyle things?" |
| **bus outing** | facility outing | "Have you been on a bus outing lately?" |

## Opener colloquialisms

| Style | Example |
|-------|---------|
| **Local-light** | "G'day, Mrs Tan… quiet yarn…" (`greeting.txt`) |
| **Standard** | "Hello, Mrs Tan… friendly chat…" — swap if resident seems formal |

Follow their cue after turn one. **Good on you** is a natural affirmation in either mode.

## CALD caution

Many residents are **not** native Australian English speakers (AIHW RAC language data; Cultural Diversity in Ageing):

- Prefer **concrete** words: meals, sleep, family, garden, visitors
- Avoid puns, rhyming slang, cemetery jokes (*Rookwood*), niche rural-only terms
- If they use Italian, Greek, Cantonese, Arabic, Vietnamese, etc., reflect in **simple English**
- Do not assume they know *yakka*, *arvo*, *RSL*, or *footy*
- Professional interpreters for complex needs — TIS **131 450** (staff arrange, not AI)

## Men's / stoic presentation

Australian research (rural stoicism; older men's mental health): may say **"I'm fine"**, **"she'll be right"**, **"no worries"**, or **"don't get lonely"** while describing **back pain, a crook knee, tiredness, crankiness, or boredom** before mood.

- Use somatic and daily-life entry (sleep, meals, garden, footy on TV)
- **One** gentle probe after minimising — do not argue
- Activity framing (garden, morning tea, bowls, men's shed) is safer than "how is your mental health?"

healthdirect: older Australians often mention **dizziness, aches and pains, poor sleep, lack of energy, not leaving the house, not eating**, and **not enjoying usual things** rather than "feeling depressed." Some avoid saying they are **not coping**.
